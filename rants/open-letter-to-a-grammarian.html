An Open Letter to a Grammarian

A warning before. I wrote this in one go, and I haven't (and don't intend to) edited or corrected this. It's up here for a laugh more than anything.

A friend of mine got a message on a comment (opinion) they wrote online - one of those people who doesn’t like something but can’t actually come up with a good reason so they just attack the grammar - going on about how the sentences were ‘tortured’ and other such pretentious crap. One of the main complaints was that they’d used ‘whom’ instead of ‘who’ once. Here’s an open letter to that commenter.

I don’t want to dox the people involved, so I won’t show the full posts here. You’ll have to take my word on this one.

Dear asshole, The English language has existed in some form or another for around 1500 years, and at no point in that period have 2 people spoken it the same way. I’m sure you’ve read Beowulf or a similarly ancient tale, some Chausser, and lots of Shakespeare in your no doubt extensive education, and I’d bet at least two of those were in translation - The English we have today is very different from the English of yesterday, and the English of tomorrow should be more different still.

As human beings, we innovate. If we see something done poorly or unnecessarily we do it better or we drop it, and that’s a good thing. Just as that’s true (and it should be!) with any aspect of our lives, so it should be with our language. If you truly don’t think that’s the case, then I wish you every success with your flint-knapping. If you do, I invite you think about why you made that comment.

Here’s the excerpt that you’re so annoyed about:

“Those whom freely did not follow him”

You were able to ‘correct’ this sentence, so you must have understood it. However, there must have been something deficient about the message and the wording that you thought would be improved by dropping that ‘m’ - is it just that it’s ‘correct’?

If you ask the Oxford people, they’d agree with you. If you asked the grammarians of the 1800s who killed singular ‘they’, or Johnathan Swift, who tried to establish an Academie Anglaise, they’d agree with you. If you asked Sir John Cheke, he’d say that “… our own tung shold be written cleane and pure”.

You might not agree with how Cheke wrote that agreement, however, and he might be rolling in his grave over how you worded your correction. I think you’re both wrong. Language is always changing and evolving, and trying to dam it simply does not work - look at the broad failure of the Academie Francais, or how standard Mandarin Chinese disenfranchises almost all of its citizens. Even if it did work (and again, it doesn’t), would that actually be a good thing? Think about why someone adopts a new feature - is it because that new feature is worse? Is it because that new feature makes them harder to understand? Do they want to sound like a degerate? Do they hate you personally? Obviously not!

People adopt a new feature, quite simply, because it’s better at something. Better at communicating an idea, better at expressing a new concept, quicker, easier to pronounce, better at signaling an identity, whatever. People drop a feature, like your precious ‘who/whom’ distinction, because it isn’t. ‘Whom’ specifically is a relic from a different language - a language that had 3 different words for ‘you’ and a declension system which was becoming redundant (hām, hāme, hāmes, hāmas etc, now just ‘home’). It has no place in today’s English, and hasn’t for decades or centuries. The only reason that you still use it is to show off how clever you are - just like the semicolon, the main reason to use ‘whom/who’ is to show off that you know how it works! Piss off.

That’ll do, I think
